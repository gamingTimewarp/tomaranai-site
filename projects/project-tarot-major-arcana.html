A System of the Tarot Major Arcana as Eschatological Archetypes

August 2026 · 134 pages

A structural reading of the Tarot. This work establishes and defends a system wherein the traditional archetypes of the Major Arcana are interpreted and identified through Judaeo-Christian eschatological figures — arrived at through a combination of historical analysis and personal revelation — and examines the individual and structural implications of those identities.

Drawing primarily from the Thoth deck while considering the most historically influential traditions, the system treats the meanings of the cards as latent rather than invented: where the Golden Dawn attributions, Qabalistic correspondences, and biblical and prophetic parallels converge on a single figure, that convergence is read not as coincidence but as recognition of a structure already present.

The Method

No single symbolic layer is treated as primary. Interpretation arises through the alignment of card iconography across traditions, Golden Dawn attributions, Qabalistic structure — particularly the Tree of Life and its paths — and the symbolic developments made explicit in the Thoth deck. Each identity is stated as a point of precise resolution rather than a reductive label, and every attribution is subjected afterward to adversarial testing: a dedicated "Defence" section for each card raises and answers the strongest objections against it.

The Arc

Read as a whole, the twenty-two Major Arcana trace a single movement from undifferentiated potential to total completion, operating simultaneously at the personal, historical, and cosmological scale:
